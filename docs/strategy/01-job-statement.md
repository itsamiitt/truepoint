# Job Statements — Market Definitions

A market = job executor + job to be done. This product serves three, and the
second one contains the insight the whole business depends on.

## Market 1 — SELLER (demand side, primary)
- Job executor: B2B sales professionals doing outbound — SDRs, AEs, founders
  selling, and recruiters (a near-identical job with candidates as "prospects").
- Core functional job: **build and maintain an accurate, reachable list of the
  right people to contact at target accounts.**
- Emotional jobs: feel confident a dial or email has a real chance before
  spending effort on it; avoid the embarrassment of pitching someone who left
  the company months ago; end the day feeling effort went into real prospects.
- Social job: be seen (by prospects and by their own manager) as a rep who does
  their homework.
- Sanity check: the statement held true in 1985 with a Rolodex and a phone
  book. No product, no adjectives, one job. ✓

## Market 2 — CONTRIBUTOR (supply side)
**There is no job called "contribute data."** No one wakes up wanting to donate
records. Treating contribution as its own job produces chore-based designs
(forms, campaigns, begging) that starve the network. Instead, contribution
must be attached — as automatic exhaust or a one-tap side effect — to jobs the
executor already has:
- **Verify a contact's reachability before spending effort on outreach** →
  every user-run verification becomes a shared verification event.
- **Keep my CRM/address book accurate and deduplicated with minimal effort** →
  a two-way sync that cleans *their* data yields anonymized field deltas back.
- **Access the data I need within my budget** → the freemium Community tier:
  keeping a contribution channel active sustains expanded free access (the
  spirit of the Jigsaw/Data.com give-to-get model, restructured as a tier
  rather than a currency).
- **Correct a wrong record the moment it burns me** → one-tap "this person
  left / wrong number" at the point of pain.
Design law derived from this: *the best contribution is one the contributor
barely notices making* (outcome C-01), and *contributing must never expose
whom they're selling to* (C-02).

## Market 3 — ADMIN / REVOPS (buyer side)
- Job executor: RevOps / sales-ops leader who owns the CRM and the tool budget.
- Core functional job: **ensure the team's outreach data is accurate,
  deduplicated, and lawful to use.**
- Emotional job: never be the person who caused a compliance incident.
- This job executor signs the contract; outcomes A-01/A-02/S-11 are their
  purchase criteria.

## Forces acting on adoption (to validate in Phase 0 interviews)
- Push: bounced sequences and dead dials wasting quota-pressured hours; CRM rot;
  incumbent tools' cost per seat; poor coverage in the team's specific
  territory or segment.
- Pull: data that is *demonstrably* fresh (visible verification recency), plus
  earning access instead of only buying it.
- Anxieties (design against these explicitly): "am I leaking my book of
  business / pipeline by contributing?" (C-02) · "is this data legal to use?"
  (C-05, A-01) · "will my admin approve an extension with these permissions?"
  (minimal-permission extension, 08/09) · "is the free tier a trap?"
- Habits (respect or import them): existing seats on incumbent tools; the
  spreadsheet of hoarded contacts; LinkedIn as the default research surface —
  which is exactly why the extension meets them there.
